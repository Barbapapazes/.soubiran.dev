The STAR interview technique is a structured method for responding to behavioral interview questions. Sometimes, it's clearly indicated that the interviewer is asking for a STAR response, but often, they are not. Even if the method isn't explicitly mentioned, using the STAR technique helps you provide clear, complete, and organized answers that demonstrate your skills and experiences effectively. I personally highly recommend using the STAR method for all behavioral questions.

> [!TIP]
> You can train your STAR responses when telling stories about something that happened to you to a friend.

## STAR Method

The STAR method is an interviewing technique used by employers to evaluate job candidates by how they respond to behavioral and situational based interview questions. Each answer is structured and must provide enough context and information that the interviewer doesn't have to ask follow-up questions.

Behavioral and situational questions are designed to assess how you have handled or would handle specific work-related situations. They often start with phrases like "Tell me about a time when..." or "How would you handle...".

STAR is an acronym that stands for:

- **Situation**: Describe the context within which you performed a task or faced a challenge at work. _Provide context! More context! It's never enough._
- **Task**: Explain the actual task or responsibility that you, and your team if applicable, had in that situation.
- **Action**: Describe the specific actions you took to address the task or challenge. Focus on what you did, rather than what the team or group did. This is your opportunity to showcase your skills and decision-making process.
- **Result**: Share the outcomes or results of your actions. This helps the interviewer understand the impact of your actions and the value you brought to the situation.

It doesn't have to be a success story nor specifically about work. It can be about a personal project, an open-source contribution, a conference talk. The key is to provide a clear and structured response that highlights your skills and experiences effectively.

It's recommended to use "I" statements. It's an interview to hire you, not your team. Also, you can mention technical details and technologies used to help the interviewer understand the context and the complexity of the situation.

> [!TIP]
> Diversify your STAR stories to cover the maximum of your skills and experiences. For each question, try to choose a different environment.

> [!TIP]
> Take your time when answering STAR questions. Long answers are completely fine in this case as long as they follow the STAR structure.

## Preparing STAR Stories

When preparing for an interview, put yourself in the interviewer's shoes to understand what they are trying to find out.

- **Situation**: What kind of environment do they want to know about? A work environment? A team environment? A conflict situation? A situation where you had to learn something new?
- **Task**: What kind of responsibility do they want to know about? A leadership role? A technical challenge? A communication challenge? A situation where you had to manage a workload?
- **Action**: What kind of actions do they want to know about? A technical solution? A communication strategy? A learning strategy? A time management strategy?
- **Result**: What kind of results do they want to know about? A successful outcome? A learning outcome? An improvement outcome?

This is essential to make sure you choose the right story and you didn't miss the point of the question.
