By clearly outlining the context, your responsibilities, the specific actions you took, and the final outcomes, you can provide comprehensive and compelling responses. Preparing diverse STAR stories in advance helps you effectively demonstrate your skills and experience to potential employers.

The STAR interview technique is a structured method for responding to behavioral interview questions. Sometimes, it's clearly indicated that the interviewer is asking for a STAR response, but often, it is not. Even if the method isn't explicitly mentioned, using the STAR technique helps you provide clear, complete, and organized answers that demonstrate your skills and experiences effectively. I highly recommend using the STAR method for all behavioral questions.

> [!TIP]
> You can practice your STAR responses by telling friends stories about things that have happened to you.

## STAR Method

The STAR method is an interviewing technique used by employers to evaluate job candidates based on how they respond to behavioral and situational interview questions. Each answer is structured and must provide enough context and information so that the interviewer doesn't have to ask follow-up questions.

Behavioral and situational questions are designed to assess how you have handled or would handle specific work-related situations. They often start with phrases like "Tell me about a time when..." or "How would you handle...".

STAR is an acronym that stands for:

- **Situation**: Describe the context within which you performed a task or faced a challenge at work. _Provide context! More context! It's never enough._
- **Task**: Explain the actual task or responsibility that you—and your team, if applicable—had in that situation.
- **Action**: Describe the specific actions you took to address the task or challenge. Focus on what you did, rather than what the team or group did. This is your opportunity to showcase your skills and decision-making process.
- **Result**: Share the outcomes or results of your actions. This helps the interviewer understand the impact of your actions and the value you brought to the situation.

Your story doesn't have to be a resounding success, nor does it have to be specifically about work. It can be about a personal project, an open-source contribution, or a conference talk. The key is to provide a clear and structured response that highlights your skills and experiences effectively.

It's recommended to use "I" statements. It's an interview to hire you, not your team. Also, you can mention technical details and the technologies used to help the interviewer understand the context and complexity of the situation.

> [!TIP]
> Diversify your STAR stories to cover as many of your skills and experiences as possible. For each question, try to choose a different environment or scenario.

> [!TIP]
> Take your time when answering STAR questions. Long answers are completely fine in this case, as long as they follow the STAR structure.

## Preparing STAR Stories

When preparing for an interview, put yourself in the interviewer's shoes to understand what they are trying to find out.

- **Situation**: What kind of environment do they want to know about? A work environment? A team environment? A conflict situation? A situation where you had to learn something new?
- **Task**: What kind of responsibility do they want to know about? A leadership role? A technical challenge? A communication challenge? A situation where you had to manage a heavy workload?
- **Action**: What kind of actions do they want to know about? A technical solution? A communication strategy? A learning strategy? A time management strategy?
- **Result**: What kind of results do they want to know about? A successful outcome? A learning outcome? An improvement outcome?

This preparation is essential to ensure you choose the right story and don't miss the point of the question.

## STAR Questions and Answers

The following are some STAR questions that you can expect in an interview. The answers are drawn from my own experience. The goal is to give you an idea of the content to include in your own STAR stories.

### Describe a situation where another team lacked key skills you depended on—how did you unblock delivery?

- **Situation**: Collaborating with the IT team in a space company.
- **Task**: Deploying a software solution for the Avionics team.
- **Action**: I worked closely with the IT team to help them acquire the necessary skills and knowledge to deploy the solution. This involved extensive training, documentation, and pair programming sessions. I also adapted my communication style and remained patient with them, as they were not used to the same tools and processes as the Avionics team.
- **Result**: After 9 months of daily work and close collaboration, we successfully deployed the solution.

### How do you respond when a project is late and stakeholders are unhappy?

- **Situation**: Delivering a broken version of the internal component library.
- **Task**: Fixing the issues and making sure to avoid similar situations in the future.
- **Action**: I quickly identified the issues and fixed them. This involved a lot of debugging, testing, communication, and pair programming with the stakeholders to keep them informed about the progress. I also implemented end-to-end testing using Playwright in a realistic mock project to ensure that the component library was working as expected and to prevent similar situations in the future.
- **Result**: Fewer regressions, fewer bugs, more confidence in the component library, and more time to develop new components since we received fewer bug reports.

### Tell me about constructive criticism you received—how did you respond and adjust?

- **Situation**: Receiving feedback about delivering too much and too quickly.
- **Task**: Adjusting my delivery style to better fit the team's needs and expectations.
- **Action**: I took a step back and reflected on the feedback. I realized that while I was delivering a lot of value, it was overwhelming for the team to keep up with the pace. I adjusted my delivery style by breaking down my work into smaller tasks and delivering them more frequently. I also communicated more with the team to understand their needs and expectations, ensuring that I was delivering value in a way that was manageable for them.
- **Result**: The team was happier with the pace of delivery, and I was able to deliver value more effectively. I also learned the importance of adapting my delivery style to fit the team's needs and expectations.

### What accomplishment are you most proud of, and why?

- **Situation**: Delivering talks at major conferences like Devoxx.
- **Task**: Sharing my knowledge and experience with the community and contributing to its growth.
- **Action**: I prepared and delivered high-quality talks that were relevant and interesting for the audience. This involved a lot of research, preparation, and practice. I also remained open to feedback and adjusted my talks accordingly to make sure they were well-received by the audience.
- **Result**: I was selected to speak at major conferences, which is a great accomplishment because it means that people trust me and value my knowledge and experience. It's also gratifying to see that my talks are well-received and that they give people ideas and inspire them to make changes.

### Tell me about a time you went above and beyond the requirements—what motivated you?

- **Situation**: Adding a new plugin to the Pinia Colada project.
- **Task**: Initially, it was just about adding a new plugin to the project, but I went on to create a dedicated package for the plugin and revamped all the existing and missing documentation to make it easier for users to use and contribute to it. It resulted in 10 pull requests, and no one had asked for it.
- **Action**: I did a lot of research to understand how the plugin worked and how to integrate it into the project. I also wrote extensive documentation to ensure that users could easily understand how to use the plugin and how to contribute to it.
- **Result**: The plugin was successfully added to the project, and the documentation was well-received by the users. The maintainer also appreciated my work and thanked me for going above and beyond the requirements.
